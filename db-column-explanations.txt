Column Explanations for DB_31_24&25.xlsb
=========================================

Purpose of this document
------------------------
This file explains the meaning and diploma-project relevance of each column found in the raw dealership sales dataset. The dataset appears to be an Excel Binary Workbook (.xlsb) containing automotive parts/service transaction records. Some columns are clearly understandable from the values and business context, while some coded columns need confirmation from the company, ERP system, or data owner.


1. FR
-----
Likely meaning:
Franchise, brand, or product group code.

Examples:
CJ, LU

Possible interpretation:
CJ may refer to Jeep/Chrysler parts. LU may refer to lubricants.

Importance:
Useful for grouping products by brand/category. In the diploma project, FR can be used for filtering, analysis, dashboard grouping, and possibly as a model feature.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium to high. Demand patterns may differ by franchise/category.


2. Part Number
--------------
Meaning:
Unique identifier/code for each spare part or product.

Examples:
CJ6AD72RXFAH, LU481371111

Importance:
This is one of the most important columns. It identifies what product was sold or returned.

Forecasting relevance:
Very high. For part-level demand forecasting, this is the main product key.


3. Description
--------------
Meaning:
Human-readable product or part name.

Examples:
MOULDING WHEEL FLARE(JL), MOBIL SUPER 3000XE 5W30 500ML

Importance:
Useful for understanding the part number and making reports/dashboard outputs readable.

Forecasting relevance:
Low to medium. Usually not used directly in the first model, but useful for interpretation and presentation.


4. WIPNo
--------
Likely meaning:
Work In Progress number, job card number, service order number, or internal transaction reference.

Importance:
Helps identify which service/job/invoice transaction the row belongs to.

Forecasting relevance:
Low. Usually not used as a forecasting feature because it is an operational ID, not a demand driver.


5. Invoice
----------
Meaning:
Invoice number or billing document number.

Note:
Excel may display large invoice numbers in scientific notation, such as 4.2E+07. Python/pandas may show the full number instead.

Importance:
Useful for tracing transactions and checking duplicates or returns.

Forecasting relevance:
Low. It is mainly an identifier, not a demand feature.


6. Date
-------
Meaning:
Transaction date.

Note:
In Python, the date may appear as an Excel serial number such as 45350. This should be converted using:
pd.to_datetime(df["Date"], origin="1899-12-30", unit="D")

Importance:
Essential for time-series analysis. It allows the data to be grouped by day, week, month, quarter, and year.

Forecasting relevance:
Very high. Forecasting is impossible without a valid date column.


7. Account
----------
Likely meaning:
Customer account code, internal account code, or buyer identifier.

Examples:
M0000193, I3101835, I3154025

Importance:
Can help identify customer behavior, repeat buyers, wholesale vs retail behavior, or internal accounts.

Forecasting relevance:
Low for v1. It may be useful in advanced customer-level demand analysis, but it adds complexity.


8. Retail Val
-------------
Meaning:
Retail/list value of the item before discount.

Importance:
Shows the standard selling value before discounting.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium. Useful for business analysis and pricing/discount studies, but not the main demand target.


9. Sale val
-----------
Meaning:
Actual sales value after discount.

Importance:
Shows the actual revenue recorded for the transaction.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium to high if the project later includes revenue forecasting. For the first demand model, quantity is more important.


10. Disc val
------------
Meaning:
Discount value.

Example:
Retail Val 1358, Sale val 883, Disc val 475.

Importance:
Shows how much value was discounted from the retail price.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium. Discounts may influence demand, but this should be used carefully because future discount values may not be known at prediction time.


11. Disc %
----------
Meaning:
Discount percentage.

Importance:
Shows the percentage discount applied to the sale.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium. Can help explain sales behavior, but may cause data leakage if future discounts are unknown.


12. Cost val
------------
Meaning:
Cost value of the sold part for the business.

Importance:
Used to understand margins and profitability.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium. Useful for business prioritization, such as focusing forecasts on high-profit parts.


13. Profit
----------
Meaning:
Profit from the transaction.

Likely formula:
Profit = Sale val - Cost val

Importance:
Important for business impact analysis. A part with lower quantity but higher profit may be more important than a high-volume low-profit item.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium. Not the main target for v1, but useful for ranking important parts.


14. PC
------
Likely meaning:
Product code, price code, profit code, or product class.

Importance:
This is a coded business field. It may represent a classification used inside the dealership or ERP system.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown until confirmed. It may become useful if it represents product category or price class.


15. Qty
-------
Meaning:
Quantity sold or returned.

Examples:
Qty = 1 means one item sold.
Qty = 16 means sixteen items sold.
Qty = -1 means a return, credit note, or reversal.

Importance:
This is the main target variable for demand forecasting.

Forecasting relevance:
Very high. Monthly demand will usually be calculated as sum of Qty by Month and Part Number.


16. DIV
-------
Likely meaning:
Division code.

Example:
31

Importance:
May identify the business division, department, or company unit.

Forecasting relevance:
Low if it has only one constant value. Medium if it contains multiple meaningful divisions.


17. BR
------
Likely meaning:
Branch code.

Examples:
41, 13

Importance:
Identifies which branch/location sold the item.

Forecasting relevance:
High if forecasting by branch. Medium if forecasting total demand across all branches.


18. D
-----
Likely meaning:
Department, document type, demand/source code, or transaction category.

Examples:
H, E, Q

Importance:
This is a coded business field and needs confirmation.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown to medium. If it identifies transaction type or department, it may help filter valid sales.


19. AT
------
Likely meaning:
Account type, activity type, or transaction type.

Examples:
I or blank

Importance:
Needs confirmation from the data owner or ERP documentation.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown. It may help separate transaction categories.


20. AC
------
Likely meaning:
Account category, activity code, or sales/transaction classification.

Examples:
U, I

Importance:
Needs confirmation. It may classify the type of transaction or customer/account.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown to medium depending on its real meaning.


21. A
-----
Likely meaning:
Action/status code, adjustment flag, or account/sales code.

Examples:
R, I

Importance:
Could be important if it identifies returns, invoice status, or transaction type.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown to medium. Needs validation before using in modeling.


22. ST
------
Likely meaning:
Status code.

Examples:
R, I

Importance:
May indicate whether the transaction is retail, internal, issued, returned, or another status.

Forecasting relevance:
Medium if it helps filter valid demand transactions.


23. MLI
-------
Likely meaning:
Main line indicator, material line indicator, or transaction line status.

Examples:
A

Importance:
Could describe the line/item status in the invoice or service system.

Forecasting relevance:
Low if constant. Medium if it separates valid/invalid transaction lines.


24. DC
------
Likely meaning:
Discount code, department code, dealer code, or damage/claim code.

Examples:
0, FA4, FE2

Importance:
This is a coded field and may explain pricing, discounting, or transaction classification.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown. Probably not needed for v1 unless it identifies important sales categories.


25. LC
------
Likely meaning:
Location code, line code, or labor code.

Examples:
0

Importance:
Needs confirmation. If it mostly contains 0, it may have low value.

Forecasting relevance:
Low unless it contains meaningful non-zero categories.


26. SC
------
Likely meaning:
Sub-code, service code, sales code, or stock code.

Examples:
FA40, FE27

Importance:
May provide more detailed classification connected to DC or service/discount logic.

Forecasting relevance:
Unknown. Not needed for the first forecasting version unless confirmed useful.


27. ACLS
--------
Likely meaning:
Account class or customer class.

Importance:
Many rows appear blank, so this column may have high missingness.

Forecasting relevance:
Low for v1 unless it contains useful customer segmentation.


28. DVC
-------
Likely meaning:
Division code, device code, discount voucher code, or dealer/vendor code.

Importance:
Many rows appear blank. Needs confirmation.

Forecasting relevance:
Low for v1 unless meaningful values exist.


29. VLP
-------
Likely meaning:
Value/price/list-related code or numeric flag.

Examples:
0.0

Importance:
If this column is mostly zero, it may not add value.

Forecasting relevance:
Low unless it has meaningful variation.


30. VNP
-------
Likely meaning:
Value/net price-related code or numeric flag.

Examples:
0.0

Importance:
If this column is mostly zero, it may not add value.

Forecasting relevance:
Low unless it has meaningful variation.


31. L
-----
Likely meaning:
Line number, line flag, or transaction line indicator.

Examples:
1

Importance:
May identify line records in the transaction file. If constant, it has little analytical use.

Forecasting relevance:
Low if constant.
